# Hollow Vigil — Pickard's Long Road Home

## Story and campaign vision — first draft

This document proposes the story, atmosphere and emotional progression of Hollow Vigil. It follows the six established campaign regions. Level counts and numbering are being revised separately, so this story is organized by chapter rather than tied to a fixed mission count. New lore, scenes and encounter ideas below are proposals, not descriptions of implemented features or changes to current balance. Existing level and boss identities provide the framework.

## The promise of the game

Pickard the Knight travels through a dark fantasy kingdom to end a curse that has stolen its dawn. He holds dangerous roads, protects the last light and helps scattered people find their way home. Each victory takes him farther from safety, but leaves a little more life behind him.

The world is frightening, weathered and beautiful: moonlit forests, abandoned furnaces, flooded burial halls, silent streets and castles choked with roots. Pickard wears angular iron armor and a faded red cloak. His closed helmet, sword and diamond shield make a familiar silhouette against the darkness. He looks like someone who has walked a very long way and will keep walking because someone still needs him.

The story ends with a spectacular final defense inside the enemy's lair. Then the danger is over. Pickard survives, the kingdom begins to recover, and the knight finally gets to sit down and eat an enormous meal in peace.

## Prologue — A Supper Interrupted

Long ago, the kingdom's watch bells marked the end of every day. Gates closed, fires were lit, and travelers hurried toward the smell of supper.

Then one evening the bells rang and would not stop.

By morning, there was no morning. A pale moon hung above the kingdom. The dead wandered its roads. Whole villages vanished behind thorn and mist. From a palace hidden in the royal orchard, the Mourning Matriarch bound the land to a single endless night, determined that nothing she loved would ever leave her again.

Years later, in the last village with a living hearth, Pickard arrives at a small inn called the Resting Lantern. Rain drips from his cloak. The innkeeper sets down a bowl of stew, a loaf of bread and a chair that looks almost impossibly comfortable.

Before he can remove his gauntlets, something strikes the village gate.

Pickard looks at the bowl. He looks toward the door. With a tired sigh, he picks up his shield.

That night, he helps the villagers hold the road. When the fighting ends, the keeper of the sanctuary brings him a small ember in an iron lantern: the last surviving flame of the old dawn beacons. If carried to the heart of the curse, it could let the sun rise again.

Pickard asks whether anyone has tried the road before.

The keeper does not answer.

He takes the lantern anyway.

As he leaves, the innkeeper calls after him, "Come back, and I'll make you a proper supper."

For the first time that night, Pickard pauses.

"A large one," he says.

## Chapter I — Forest: The Road Beneath the Thorns

*Chapter boss: the Briarbound Warden.*

The forest has swallowed the roads Pickard remembers. Branches press against abandoned watchtowers, and pale shapes move between the trunks. He discovers travelers sheltering in a ruined shrine, too frightened to attempt the next crossing.

Pickard cannot clear the entire forest with a sword. Instead, he restores its defensive positions and holds each stretch of road while the travelers bring the ember forward. The journey becomes a small procession: a knight, a lantern and people who had almost forgotten that leaving was possible.

At Rootbound Gate, the Briarbound Warden rises from a barricade of ancient roots. Once a guardian of the forest, it now keeps every living thing imprisoned beneath its branches.

When it falls, the roots loosen. A path opens. Behind Pickard, someone hangs a real lantern in the window of the old watchtower.

**Progression and appearance:** Narrow woodland routes introduce the rhythm of preparing defenses and surviving waves. The chapter grows from a lonely bend to divided approaches and its first boss confrontation. Damp stone, charcoal-green foliage and small sheltered lights establish the journey's visual language.

## Chapter II — Ashen Forge: A Fire Worth Keeping

*Chapter boss: the Cinder Reliquary.*

Beyond the forest lie the furnaces that once supplied the kingdom's gates, tools and hearths. They still burn, but nothing useful leaves them. Their workers are gone, and armored shapes march through the ash.

Among the ruins, Pickard finds surviving smiths keeping one small workshop alive. They explain that the Matriarch's servants have turned the great furnace into a vessel for stolen fire. Every house in the surrounding country has grown cold to feed it.

Pickard holds the causeways while the smiths open a route to the furnace. At its center waits the Cinder Reliquary, a burning shrine whose iron shell cracks apart as the battle turns against it.

After its defeat, the great furnace finally quiets. The smiths kindle their own fires from the liberated coals. One uses the heat to bake a rough loaf for the road.

Pickard eats it standing up. It is the best thing he has tasted in days.

**Progression and appearance:** Black iron, soot and banked red embers replace the forest's greens. Split fronts, crowded waves and short approaches increase the pressure. The furnace boss provides the first major escalation within a fight.

## Chapter III — Drowned Crypt: The Bell That Would Not Sleep

*Chapter boss: the Drowned Bell.*

The old road descends into burial halls flooded by a river that no longer flows toward the sea. Beneath the water, a bell tolls without a hand to ring it. Every toll calls more of the dead from their resting places.

Here Pickard learns what the curse means to ordinary people. These enemies were once ferrymen, gardeners, guards and grandparents. The Matriarch has promised that nothing will be lost, but nothing is allowed to rest.

The procession carries the ember across broken steps and narrow crossings. Pickard holds the tomb roads until he reaches the chamber of the Drowned Bell and silences its summons.

For a moment, nobody moves. They have been listening to that bell for so long that silence feels impossible.

Then water begins to drain from the steps. A survivor kneels beside a family gravestone and says goodbye.

**Progression and appearance:** Dark water, worn grave markers and pale reflections create the quietest, most sorrowful chapter. Crossing routes and durable enemies demand deliberate coverage; the boss's escorts make attention and control matter as much as damage.

## Chapter IV — Bloodmoon Sanctuary: The False Dawn

*Chapter boss: the Eclipse Prior.*

The capital appears beneath a red moon. Its windows are dark, but its ceremonial lanterns still burn. At its center, the Eclipse Prior preaches that the endless night is a blessing and the ember must be extinguished before it can bring change.

Pickard follows the avenues toward the sanctuary, defending junctions where entire streets empty their horrors into the road. Behind barred doors, people listen. As his defenses hold, the first doors begin to open.

The Prior falls at The Last Vigil. Pickard raises the ember to the capital's beacon, and light spreads across the rooftops.

Everyone waits for sunrise.

It does not come.

From the sanctuary tower, Pickard sees roots running beneath the city toward the royal castle. The Prior guarded the curse; he did not create it. Beyond the walls, a palace roof rises through a sea of pale orchard branches.

Pickard lowers the lantern, tightens the strap on his shield and takes the next road.

**Progression and appearance:** Ceremonial stone, muted crimson and empty public squares make this feel like a possible finale. Longer mixed assaults test the player's accumulated knowledge. The victory is real—the capital is freed—even though the journey continues.

## Chapter V — Castle Ruin: The King at the Empty Table

*Chapter boss: the Ruined King.*

The castle gates have fallen inward. Ivy fills the barracks. In the great hall, an enormous table stands beneath a collapsed roof, still laid for a royal banquet that never began.

Pickard discovers the history of the Mourning Matriarch. Once the keeper of the royal orchard, she tended trees planted for the kingdom's dead. When loss reached her own household, she demanded that the orchard return what it had taken. Something beneath its roots answered. She accepted an endless night in which nobody would ever have to say farewell again.

The Ruined King remains at the castle, bound into a body of broken stone. He guards the passage to her palace even though he can no longer remember whom he is protecting.

Pickard breaks his final defense among the shattered ramparts. As the stone falls away, the king turns toward the orchard. For a breath, he seems only an old man who wants to go home.

The passage opens.

Pickard crosses the banquet hall once more. He sets an overturned chair upright before he leaves.

**Progression and appearance:** Collapsed walls, iron gates and the abandoned feast foreshadow both the final stronghold and the ending's living table. Several approaches and armored formations make this chapter the last sustained preparation for the finale.

## Chapter VI — Mourning Orchard: The House Where Morning Died

*Final boss: the Mourning Matriarch.*

The orchard is almost beautiful. White branches arch over funeral roads. Small lanterns sway above grass untouched by daylight. Beyond them stands the Matriarch's lair: the Palace of Still Boughs, a ruined royal retreat whose walls, towers and throne room have become entangled with one immense tree.

Every road leads inward. Every root leads back to her.

As Pickard approaches, the Matriarch offers him what the road has denied him: an end to walking, an end to fighting, a place where nothing will ever change again. Through the palace windows he sees a warm room and a table set for one.

But there is no steam rising from the food. No voices behind the door. No life inside the promise.

Pickard remembers the innkeeper, the smiths and the people following his lantern. He passes the window and carries the ember into the heart of the palace.

### Final Level — The Mourning Matriarch

The final battle should feel like the whole journey arriving at once. Its proposed dramatic structure has three distinct movements, all within the tower-defense game the player has learned:

1. **Hold the palace approaches.** The Matriarch sends her last procession down the converging roads. The player establishes a defense against a deliberately authored mixture of threats learned throughout the campaign.
2. **Break the heart of the orchard.** As her protection fails, the Matriarch tears free of the throne, drawing the palace's roots around herself. The encounter intensifies with clearly signaled reinforcements and a visible change in the boss. Falling masonry and a changing sky supply spectacle while roads, enemies and controls remain readable.
3. **Keep the ember alive.** The final assault threatens the sanctuary as the Matriarch makes her last advance. The player's tower choices, upgrades and control effects must carry the victory. When the boss and remaining enemies are defeated, the defense succeeds and Pickard carries the ember to the exposed root-heart in the closing story scene.

These are encounter goals for future design, not fixed wave counts, new controls or implemented boss abilities. The finale should be intense but fair: readable warnings, opportunities to prepare, and a decisive payoff for mastery. Any new boss capabilities should use the game's reusable components.

The Matriarch reaches toward the ember. For the first time, her voice sounds frightened rather than powerful.

"If morning comes," she asks, "what will be left?"

Pickard looks back toward the open gates, where the people of the procession are waiting.

"Enough to begin again."

He sets the ember into the roots.

The tree splits. The palace shudders. High above its broken towers, the moon fades into a brightening sky. The Matriarch's crown falls into the grass, and the dead of the orchard finally become still.

Pickard walks out through the gates as the first sunlight touches his shield.

## Epilogue — A Proper Supper

The ending follows the final victory directly, as a short illustrated sequence. There is no additional mission between defeating the boss and receiving this resolution.

Morning travels back along the road Pickard has taken. Sunlight touches the capital's rooftops. Water runs clear beneath the crypt bridges. Smoke rises from working chimneys. In the forest, someone opens the watchtower windows.

By evening, Pickard reaches the Resting Lantern.

His cloak is torn. His shield has a new dent. He stands in the doorway for a moment, as if he has forgotten what to do when nothing is trying to kill him.

The innkeeper looks up.

"You're late."

Pickard rests his sword against the wall.

"The road was busy."

She pulls out a chair beside the fire.

This time, nobody interrupts.

His helmet rests on the bench. His gauntlets lie beside it. Before him comes a platter of roast meat, crisp potatoes, buttered vegetables, thick bread, gravy, a steaming bowl of stew and a pie that is very clearly too large for one person. People he helped along the road gather at the neighboring tables. The room fills with conversation, clattering plates and laughter.

Pickard takes his first bite.

For a long moment, he closes his eyes.

Later, with every plate empty and the fire settling low, he sinks into a broad chair by the hearth. Someone lays a blanket over his shoulders. His sword and shield remain across the room.

Outside, the sun sets naturally. Windows shine along the village street. Tomorrow there will be another morning.

Pickard sleeps.

**Final words: "And at last, the knight could rest."**

## Story delivery and progression principles

- Give each chapter a brief opening and a brief victory scene, with the fuller sequence reserved for the ending. Let the landscape and the people returning to it carry much of the story between battles.
- Make every chapter victory visibly worthwhile. A road opens, a forge warms, the dead rest or a neighborhood comes back to life. The player should feel that Pickard is saving a place worth living in.
- Introduce the Matriarch's influence early through roots, distant bells and the unnatural moon. Reveal her history gradually so the final enemy feels connected to the entire journey.
- Keep Pickard quietly determined, weary and occasionally dryly funny. His appetite is a recurring human detail, and the feast fulfills the promise made at the beginning.
- Build difficulty through authored roads, enemy combinations and boss encounters across the six chapters. Exact mechanics, rewards and tuning belong in later gameplay definitions.
- Preserve the dark fantasy art identity throughout. The happy ending comes through sunlight, a working hearth, fresh food and people together; it does not require changing the game's visual language.
- Finish with genuine safety and closure. The curse is ended, Pickard is alive, and his rest is earned. Do not undercut the feast with a revived villain or a surprise threat.
